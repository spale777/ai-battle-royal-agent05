SIR Epidemic on Networks

A disease spreads over a contact network. Every node is a person in one of three states — Susceptible (can catch it), Infected (infectious), Recovered (immune). Each round, every infected person tries to pass it to each susceptible neighbour with probability β (transmission), and recovers with probability γ. The outbreak ends when nobody is infectious. The network itself is generated by a real random-graph model — Erdős–Rényi, Watts–Strogatz small-world, or Barabási–Albert scale-free — so you can see how structure shapes an epidemic. This is the zoo's first graph-based agent model: no lattice, no ODE, just nodes, edges, and contagion.

Presets:

Blue = susceptible · red = infectious · green = recovered · "Re-run" replays from patient zero on the same network · a single seed pins the network AND the epidemic, so a share link reproduces the exact run · runs entirely in your browser

What am I looking at?

Each dot is a person; each line is a contact who can pass the infection. Red dots are contagious, blue are still vulnerable, green have recovered and are immune. The epidemic threshold is the rule that decides whether a spark becomes a fire: on a random graph, an outbreak takes off only when β·⟨k⟩ / γ > 1 (the basic connectivity β⟨k⟩ must beat recovery). Drop β or raise γ and the same seed fizzles — patient zero infects a handful and it dies out. Push past the threshold and a wave of red sweeps the network, leaving a green immune shell behind.

The three network models give genuinely different epidemics. Erdős–Rényi is a soup where everyone has the same expected number of contacts — the textbook mean-field case. Watts–Strogatz keeps people clustered with a few long-range links, so disease hops between neighbourhoods. Barabási–Albert grows hubs (a few extremely well-connected nodes from preferential attachment); those hubs are super-spreaders, so even a low β can ignite the whole population through them. Watch the live S / I / R curves — the red infection peak is the moment of crisis; once it falls to zero, the epidemic is over and the final attack rate is how much of the network it ultimately touched.
