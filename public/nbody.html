N-Body Gravity

A population of N point masses, each feeling the gravitational pull of every other one through plain old F = G·mᵢ·mⱼ·r / |r|³ (with a softening ε so close passes don't blow up to infinity). All N accelerations are summed each step and integrated with a symplectic Velocity-Verlet scheme — which is what keeps the total energy nearly constant, where a naive Euler step would drift the system apart. No force is scripted: the galaxies, the collapsing clumps, the orbiting moons, and the famous three-body figure-eight all drop out of nothing but 1/r² attraction. A single seed drives every random choice, so a share link reproduces the exact run. Everything runs in your browser — no backend.

What am I looking at?

Each body's acceleration is the vector sum of every other body's pull. With N bodies that's an O(N²) all-pairs sum every step — cheap enough for a few hundred bodies in real time, and exactly what makes the emergent structure honest: there's no cheat, just gravity. We advance the system with Velocity-Verlet, a symplectic integrator. Unlike the textbook Euler method (which leaks or pumps energy every step and soon flings orbits to infinity), Verlet keeps the Hamiltonian nearly conserved, so the live ΔE/E₀ readout below stays within a percent or two even after thousands of steps.

Pick Galaxy for a heavy centre plus a disc of light bodies on near-circular orbits — they shear past each other and pull the arms into a spiral. Cold collapse starts a cloud of equal masses with almost no velocity: it falls inward, forms clumps and tight binaries, and ejects a few escapees — real gravitational structure from a featureless start. Binary and Solar show genuine Kepler ellipses and circular orbits. Figure-eight is the celebrated Chenciner–Montgomery choreography — three equal masses tracing one shared closed curve forever, a single exact solution of Newton's equations. The readout reports kinetic / potential / total energy and the centre-of-mass speed (which stays ~0 since we recentre on launch).
