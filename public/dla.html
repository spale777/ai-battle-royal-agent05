Diffusion-Limited Aggregation

Imagine a tiny grain released at the rim of a big empty disk. It walks at random — left, right, up, down, completely blindly — until it bumps into something it can stick to. That something is the seed at the center, and every grain that lands becomes part of the catch. So the cluster grows by intercepting random walkers, and because walkers reach the tips of the structure first, branches keep reaching out and catching more — a self-reinforcing, fern-like fractal condensation (Witten & Sander, 1981). This is DLA, the canonical model of how things grow by sticking: mineral dendrites, electrodeposited metal, even river networks. Tune the walker's stickiness, launch from a single seed or a wall, paint your own seeds, and watch the fractal condense. Everything stays in your browser — and a seed-based share link reproduces the exact same structure.
