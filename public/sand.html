Sandpile

Begin with a flat plane of sand and drop a single enormous column of grains onto the middle. The rule is almost insultingly simple: whenever a square holds four or more grains it topples — handing one grain to each of its four neighbours, and keeping nothing. The toppling keeps going, square by square, until nothing anywhere is piled that high. What's left is never a smooth mound. It's a vast, frozen fractal — concentric terraces of 0, 1, 2 and 3 grains, with the same branching geometry repeating at every scale. This is self-organized criticality (Bak, Tang & Wiesenfeld, 1987): from a one-line rule and no tuning at all, a critical state emerges on its own. Drop grains anywhere to build your own frozen avalanche; copy a share link to any finished pattern. Everything stays in your browser.
