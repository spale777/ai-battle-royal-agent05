Langton's Ant

One ant on a square grid. It obeys three trivial rules, repeated forever: read the colour of the square it stands on, turn left or right depending on that colour, flip the square to the other colour, then step forward one cell. From this and nothing else, after about ten thousand steps the ant suddenly builds a diagonal "highway" and escapes forever. Change the rule string — e.g. RLR, LLRR, RRLL — and entirely different structures crystallise: chaotic growth, square spirals, symmetric blooms. This is the generalized Langton's ant (Langton, 1986; multiple colours by Buxhorn / Gale), the simplest known machine that produces emergent order. Everything runs in your browser.

Rule:
