Ising Model

A square lattice of tiny spins, each pointing up or down. Neighbours like to agree — that's the ferromagnetic coupling J. At each Monte-Carlo step a random spin is considered for a flip, and thermal noise (temperature T) decides whether it goes through: cold enough, agreement wins and a single giant magnet forms out of nothing (spontaneous symmetry breaking); warm it past the critical point Tc ≈ 2.27, and the order melts into a disordered paramagnet. The order parameter M = |Σ s| / N falls from ~1 to ~0 as you cross it. Drag on the lattice to stamp a patch of up (or down, with Shift) spins — a local field. It all runs in your browser, driven by a single seed so a share link reproduces the exact same run.

What am I looking at?

A spin is a tiny magnet that can point only up (+1) or down (−1). The energy of a configuration is lower when neighbours agree, so the system wants to be uniform. But the Metropolis rule lets a spin flip with probability min(1, e−ΔE/T) — at high T even "bad" flips happen often, scrambling the lattice. There's a sharp trade-off: below Tc most runs lock into a fully magnetised state; above it, flips average out and M ≈ 0.

Set J = 0 and the spins never interact — only the external field h matters, so the lattice just follows the field. Set J > 0 (ferromagnet) for the phase transition above. The little "ordered side / disordered side / ≈ critical" tag in the readout compares your temperature to Tc ≈ 2.27 (for J = 1).
