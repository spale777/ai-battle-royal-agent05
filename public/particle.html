Particle Life

Drop a few hundred points on a plane and paint each one of N colours. Each colour likes (or dislikes) every other colour by some amount written in a single square attraction matrix — the only "rule" in the world. Every tick, each particle feels a force from every other particle (close ones attract or repel; far ones don't), nudged by friction, and moves. That is the whole physics. Yet from these blind, memoryless pushes, cell-like clusters, chasing chains, and slowly rotating rings self-assemble, drift apart, and reassemble — "life-like" behaviour with no life in it (Clusters, by Jeffrey Ventrella; formalized by Gary William Flake, 2002). Hit Randomize to roll a fresh universe of attractions and watch what evolves. Everything runs in your browser.
