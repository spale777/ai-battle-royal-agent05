Chladni Plate / Cymatics

A square plate vibrates in a standing wave: every point (x, y) bobs with an amplitude given by a superposition of plate modes, and the points where the amplitude is zero — the nodal lines — stay still. Sprinkle sand on the plate and the grains bounce wherever the plate is loud, but the instant they wander onto a quiet nodal line they barely move and stay put. After a few thousand bounces the sand has painted the figure. The pattern is not drawn anywhere; it emerges from grains escaping the loud parts and getting trapped in the silent ones. This is a stochastic aggregation model — a different mechanism from the cellular and ODE sims in the zoo. A single seed drives every bounce, so a share link reproduces the exact same settling.

What am I looking at?

Each dot is a grain. Every frame it takes a random step whose size is proportional to the local loudness of the plate — big jittery jumps where the standing wave is at full swing, tiny steps where it is near zero. Because loud regions keep flinging grains away and quiet regions let them sit still, the population piles up on the nodal lines and the figure appears. Watch the % settled on nodes climb past 80%: that is the moment most grains have found a home on a silent line.

The four field types give genuinely different geometries. Cross superposes two orthogonal standing waves; Diagonal rotates the pattern 45° for X-shaped lattices; Concentric rings uses a radial cosine from the plate centre; Superposed adds a second orthogonal pair with independent mode numbers for moiré-like interference. Drag the mode sliders and whole new families of figures open up — this is the classic "Chladni figure" explorer, running entirely in your browser with no backend.
